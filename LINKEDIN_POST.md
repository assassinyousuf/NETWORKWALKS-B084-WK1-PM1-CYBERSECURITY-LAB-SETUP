🚀 Week 1 of my Cybersecurity Journey with NetworkWalks: Why I chose Bare-Metal over Virtualization! 💻🔐

Most cybersecurity lab setups begin with the traditional route: installing VirtualBox or VMware on Windows. 

While virtual machines have their place, for my Week 1 Project at NetworkWalks Academy, I decided to take performance and realism a step further: I installed Kali Linux Rolling (2026.3) directly on bare-metal hardware!

Here is why running Kali Linux natively on my Lenovo LOQ 15IAX9 laptop is a game-changer:

⚡ 1. Direct Wi-Fi Card Control (wlan0):
No more virtual USB pass-through drops or driver translation bottlenecks. Native hardware access unlocks raw 802.11 monitor mode and packet injection capabilities right out of the box.

⚡ 2. 100% Compute Power for Password Cracking:
All 12 cores of my 12th Gen Intel Core i5-12450HX and 24 GB of high-speed DDR5 RAM are dedicated to security tasks—with full GPU acceleration ready for Hashcat and John the Ripper.

⚡ 3. High-Throughput NVMe I/O:
Zero hypervisor disk overhead when traversing multi-gigabyte wordlists (like rockyou.txt) or analyzing massive PCAP packet captures in Wireshark.

⚡ 4. Low-Latency Raw Socket Networking:
Direct kernel-level packet transmission for rapid Nmap port scanning (epoll engine) with sub-3ms gateway latency.

🛠️ What I Built & Tested in Week 1:
✅ Native bare-metal partitioning and UEFI installation on NVMe storage
✅ NetworkManager routing and high-speed DNS configuration (1.1.1.1 & 8.8.8.8)

✅ Core pentesting toolchain audit: Nmap 7.991, Wireshark, Burp Suite, Metasploit, Gobuster, Hydra, John the Ripper, and Aircrack-ng
✅ Developed an automated bash verification script (`verify_lab.sh`) to audit system health in seconds!

Overcoming real hurdles—such as tuning aggressive Wi-Fi power-save sleep latency in NetworkManager—was one of the best learning experiences of this setup.
